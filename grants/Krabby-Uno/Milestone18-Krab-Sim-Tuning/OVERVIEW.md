# Patina Foundation Grant - Krabby-Uno Milestone 18: RL Gait Reward Tuning (In-Sim)

## Grant Overview
Take the staged curriculum in `krabby-research/parkour/parkour_tasks/parkour_tasks/crab_hexapod_task` — currently ending at the 2b2 teacher (`runs/2026-05-26_21-46-37/model_6300.pt`) and the distilled student (`runs/2026-05-26_22-57-01/model_9800.pt`) — and fix the gait, entirely in simulation, before any hardware deployment. The current policies technically locomote but the gait is bad: a "tippy-tap" micro-stepping pattern (rapid, tiny steps with almost no swing phase), no lateral or yaw command capability at all, and untested behavior under payload. The work has four substantive pieces. First, diagnose the gait quantitatively: build an instrumented eval harness that runs the bundled teacher and student checkpoints on `Flat-Walk-v0` and the 2b2-mixed `Teacher-v0`/`Student-v0` terrain and logs per-foot contact schedules, air time, stride length, foot clearance, joint torque/velocity profiles, and command-tracking error, then audit every reward stage in `config/crab_hex/agents/parkour_mdp_cfg.py` against those measurements. Two suspects are already visible in the config and must be confirmed with data: the feet-air-time rewards use `threshold: 0.05` (50 ms), which a micro-step trivially clears; and the command model is structurally forward-only — `lin_vel_x` sampled at (0.30, 0.65)/(0.45, 0.85), `heading` locked to (0.0, 0.0) under P-control, and `penalty_lin_vel_y` at weight −3.0 actively punishing sideways motion. Second, reshape the reward for gait quality: rework the air-time terms, add stride-length and contact-schedule (tripod) regularity terms, and retune the existing clearance/energy terms, implemented as new reward stage classes following the existing subclass pattern (`CrabHexTeacherBridgeRewardsCfg` → `CrabHexStage2BPhase1RewardsCfg` → ...) and trained by resuming from the 2b2 `6300` checkpoint per the curriculum's established resume discipline. Third, restore omnidirectional motion: replace the heading-locked, lateral-penalized command model with true (vx, vy, ωz) command sampling, retire `penalty_lin_vel_y`, and retrain until the policy tracks arbitrary velocity commands within tolerance across a systematic grid eval. Fourth, harden the gait against payload mass/CoM variation and a light terrain curriculum (reusing the existing sub-terrain machinery, not Stage 4 full parkour), then produce the milestone deliverable: retrained teacher and student checkpoints whose sim gait — scored on the instrumented metrics from the first task — shows proper swing phases, tripod regularity, omnidirectional tracking, and graceful degradation under load, captured as side-by-side video against the current `9800` student with the full metric report. These weights, committed to `runs/` with a README per the repo convention, become the baseline the sim-to-real milestone deploys.

## Why is this Important?
- The sim-to-real milestone reproduces on hardware whatever gait exists in sim; deploying the current tippy-tap gait means faithfully transferring a bad gait. Fixing gait quality in sim first is strictly cheaper — sim iterations cost minutes, hardware iterations cost sessions.
- The tippy-tap pattern is high-frequency, low-displacement actuation — the worst possible duty cycle for lead-screw actuators (constant direction reversals, high current, negligible travel). A proper swing-phase gait is a hardware-longevity requirement, not just an aesthetic one.
- The current command model cannot express lateral or yaw motion at all — sideways velocity is penalized at −3.0 and heading is fixed at zero — so the controller-driven demo in the sim-to-real milestone, which presumes a policy that follows a command stream, has nothing to drive. This milestone builds the command interface that demo depends on.
- Attributing each pathology to a specific reward/config term (rather than blind reward tweaking) extends the repo's existing per-stage documentation discipline (README appendices C–G) with a measured cause→fix record that every future retrain — including the prismatic-action-space retrain and the domain-randomization retrain in the sim-to-real milestone — inherits.
- Load robustness in sim de-risks the real robot's mass uncertainty: the physical krab is ~83 kg and the sim mass model is approximate, so a policy that only walks at exactly the nominal mass will not survive contact with reality.

## Tasks
Full acceptance criteria and implementation detail live in the task files in this folder (`Task1-Gait-Diagnosis.md` through `Task4-Load-Terrain-Robustness.md`). Summaries:

### Task 1 - Gait diagnosis and reward audit
#### Narrative
Before touching any reward term, measure what the current policies actually do and why. Build an instrumented eval harness in `crab_hexapod_task/scripts/` (alongside the existing `verify_crab_joint_drive.py` / `verify_crab_contact_physics.py` verification scripts) that plays the bundled checkpoints — teacher `6300` on `Teacher-v0` (2b2 mode) and student `9800` on `Student-v0` — over a fixed, versioned eval scenario set and logs, per episode: per-foot contact schedules for all 6 footpads (the `.*_Footpad` contact sensors already track air time via `track_air_time=True` in `crab_hex_scene_cfg.py`), per-foot air time and stride length distributions, swing-phase foot clearance, joint torque and velocity profiles per DOF, base velocity vs. commanded velocity (tracking error in x, y, yaw), and base orientation stability. Render the contact schedule as a gait diagram so tripod regularity (or its absence) is visible at a glance.

Then audit every reward stage in `config/crab_hex/agents/parkour_mdp_cfg.py` — `CrabHexFlatWalkRewardsCfg`, `CrabHexTeacherBridgeRewardsCfg`, `CrabHexStage2BPhase1RewardsCfg`, `CrabHexStage2BPhase2RewardsCfg`, and the command/terrain setup in `crab_hex_env_cfg.py` — against the measurements. Every reward term, its weight, and every command-sampling range gets a line in the audit: what it rewards, what the measured behavior shows, and whether it is a suspected cause of a pathology. Two suspects are already flagged from config inspection and must be confirmed or refuted with data:

- **Air-time threshold:** `reward_feet_air_time_positive` (flat-walk, weight 0.40) and `reward_feet_air_time_on_flat` (bridge, weight 0.5; zeroed by 2b2) both use `threshold: 0.05` — 50 ms, which a micro-step trivially clears, making the tippy-tap pattern reward-neutral or reward-positive. Confirm by comparing the measured air-time distribution against the threshold.
- **Forward-only command model:** `lin_vel_x` is sampled at (0.30, 0.65) in flat-walk and (0.45, 0.85) in the teacher modes, `heading` is locked to (0.0, 0.0) with `heading_control_stiffness = 1.5`, and `penalty_lin_vel_y` carries weight −3.0 in the flat-walk and bridge stacks — the policy was never commanded laterally or in yaw, and was actively punished for lateral velocity. Confirm the measured lateral/yaw tracking behavior matches (i.e., commands off the trained axis are ignored or fought).

The output is a diagnosis document: each observed pathology mapped to its suspected config cause, with the metric evidence attached, in the style of the README's per-stage appendices. No retraining happens in this task.

#### Acceptance Criteria
- Instrumented eval harness committed to `crab_hexapod_task/scripts/`; runs a specified checkpoint on a fixed, versioned eval scenario set covering `Flat-Walk-v0` and 2b2-mixed terrain; emits all listed metrics plus a rendered gait diagram per episode.
- Baseline metric reports for teacher `6300` and student `9800` captured and committed; these are the before-side of every before/after comparison in the milestone.
- Full audit table across all reward stage classes in `parkour_mdp_cfg.py` and the command/DR setup in `crab_hex_env_cfg.py`: term, weight per stage, intended effect, measured effect, pathology attribution (if any).
- The 50 ms air-time threshold hypothesis confirmed or refuted with the measured air-time distribution; finding documented.
- The forward-only command model's effect confirmed with measured off-axis tracking behavior; finding documented.
- Diagnosis document committed: every observed gait pathology mapped to a specific config cause or explicitly marked cause-unknown.

### Task 2 - Reward shaping for gait quality
#### Narrative
Fix the pathologies Task 1 attributed to reward terms, one change at a time, with the instrumented eval run after each change so every term's effect is measured rather than assumed. Changes are implemented as new reward stage classes following the repo's existing subclass pattern (a new `KRABBY_HEX_TEACHER_MODE` and/or new cfg class extending the 2b2 stack) rather than mutating the bundled stages, and training resumes from the 2b2 `6300` checkpoint per the curriculum's resume discipline — the existing stages and their bundled checkpoints stay reproducible. The expected changes (subject to Task 1 findings):

- **Air-time reward:** raise the 50 ms threshold/target to a value consistent with a real swing phase for this platform's stride timing, and shape the reward so longer proper swings beat many micro-steps. Note the 2b2 stack currently zeroes the air-time term entirely — decide, with data, whether the fixed term returns to the obstacle-walk stack or stays flat-walk-only.
- **Stride length:** a new term rewarding per-step displacement so the policy prefers fewer, longer steps over high-frequency shuffling.
- **Contact-schedule regularity:** reward tripod-gait structure (alternating sets of 3 footpads in stance) or, at minimum, penalize degenerate schedules. Partial machinery exists — `penalty_excess_feet_contact_forward` (−0.20) and the currently-zeroed `reward_stance_support_feet_when_forward` — but nothing enforces tripod phasing; this is the hexapod-specific term the quadruped-derived reward set does not provide.
- **Clearance retune:** the 2b2 lift stack (`reward_foot_clearance` 2.0, `penalty_swing_min_clearance` −0.4, `reward_swing_vertical_vel` 0.8) was tuned for obstacle lifting, not gait quality; retune against the new air-time/stride terms so the terms cooperate rather than fight.
- **Energy/smoothness:** retune `reward_torques` (−1e-5), `reward_action_rate` (−0.1), `reward_dof_acc` (−2.5e-7), and `reward_delta_torques` (−1e-7) to kill the rapid direction reversals that make the tippy-tap pattern so hostile to lead-screw hardware; current weights demonstrably don't.

Each term change gets retrained (teacher first; student re-distilled only after the reward set stabilizes — no need to re-distill on every intermediate iteration) and evaluated against the Task 1 baseline. Terms that don't measurably improve their target metric get removed, not left in "just in case." The result is a documented, minimal reward stage where every term has an attached before/after metric justifying it.

#### Acceptance Criteria
- New reward stage class(es) committed to `parkour_mdp_cfg.py` following the existing subclass pattern; bundled stages and checkpoints untouched and still reproducible.
- Air-time reward reworked; measured air-time distribution shifts to real swing phases; before/after distributions committed; the flat-walk-vs-obstacle-stack placement decision documented with data.
- Stride-length term implemented; measured stride length improves against baseline; before/after metrics committed.
- Contact-schedule term implemented; gait diagrams show recognizable tripod (or documented alternative) regularity; before/after diagrams committed.
- Clearance and energy/smoothness terms retuned; measured torque and action-rate profiles show reduced high-frequency reversals; before/after profiles committed.
- Tippy-tap pattern eliminated in the retrained policy, demonstrated on the Task 1 eval set with the gait diagram and air-time metrics.
- Final reward config committed with a per-term changelog: what changed from the 2b2 stack, why, and the metric delta each change produced; terms tried and removed are listed with the reason.
- Teacher retrained (resumed from `6300`) and student re-distilled on the final reward set; weights committed to `runs/` with a README per repo convention; forward-locomotion performance at or above the `6300`/`9800` baseline (gait fix must not cost basic capability).

### Task 3 - Omnidirectional command tracking
#### Narrative
Make the policy actually follow a command stream in all directions. Per the Task 1 finding, the current command model is structurally forward-only: `lin_vel_x`-only sampling, heading locked to (0.0, 0.0) under P-control rather than a commanded yaw rate, and lateral velocity actively penalized at −3.0. This task replaces that model with true (vx, vy, ωz) command sampling and retrains until the policy tracks arbitrary commands within a documented tolerance.

Specifics: define the command envelope (max forward/backward speed, max lateral speed, max yaw rate) from what the platform's actuators can plausibly deliver, not an arbitrary round number; replace the heading-P-control setup in `crab_hex_env_cfg.py` with sampled yaw-rate commands (the `track_ang_vel_z_exp` term already exists but has only ever seen the P-controller's output, not commanded yaw); retire `penalty_lin_vel_y` and let `track_lin_vel_xy_exp` do its job on a nonzero vy range; sample the full envelope during training including mixed commands (forward + yaw arcs, diagonal translation); and weight the tracking reward so lateral and yaw error cost comparably to forward error — the bridge-era forward-progress and heading-error terms (`reward_forward_progress_along_command`, `penalty_body_heading_error_l2`, `penalty_backward_along_command`) are all forward-axis-privileged and must be reworked or retired for the omnidirectional stage. Build a command-tracking eval that sweeps the envelope systematically — a grid of commanded (vx, vy, ωz) points, each scored on steady-state tracking error and on gait quality using the Task 2 metrics, because omnidirectional motion that reintroduces tippy-tap sideways is a regression, not a win.

Hexapods are mechanically well-suited to lateral and rotational motion — there is no platform excuse for a forward-only policy. The expected outcome is a policy that treats the command vector as the interface, which is exactly what the sim-to-real milestone's Bluetooth controller demo assumes.

#### Acceptance Criteria
- Command envelope documented (vx, vy, ωz ranges) with the actuator-based justification for each limit.
- Command model in `crab_hex_env_cfg.py` reworked: sampled vy and ωz ranges, heading-P-control replaced with commanded yaw rate for the omnidirectional stage; `penalty_lin_vel_y` retired; forward-axis-privileged bridge terms reworked or retired; all changes documented in the audit-table format from Task 1.
- Training command sampling covers the full envelope including mixed commands; sampling config committed.
- Command-tracking eval committed: grid sweep over the envelope, per-point steady-state tracking error and Task 2 gait metrics.
- Retrained policy tracks commands across the grid within documented tolerance for each axis; tracking-error heatmap over the (vx, vy, ωz) grid committed.
- Gait quality metrics from Task 2 hold during lateral, yaw, and mixed motion (no tippy-tap regression off-axis); metrics committed.
- Teacher and student retrained; weights committed to `runs/` with a README; forward performance within the Task 2 ballpark.

### Task 4 - Load and terrain robustness, and the in-sim gait demo
#### Narrative
Harden the gait against the two variations the real robot will impose immediately — mass it wasn't trained at, and ground that isn't a perfect plane — and produce the milestone deliverable. This is *not* the full domain-randomization pass (motor strength, latency, sensor noise, camera pose belong to the sim-to-real milestone, which owns the Extreme Parkour randomization port), and it is *not* Stage 4 full parkour (which stays TODO per the README's own warning against jumping to `full`). This task randomizes only the gait-relevant physical parameters:

- **Payload:** extend the existing event/DR machinery (the 2b2 stage already runs moderate push/mass/CoM randomization inherited from the go2 `EventCfg`) with added-mass and CoM-shift ranges chosen around the real robot's mass uncertainty relative to the sim model, so the policy learns to rebalance rather than assume nominal mass.
- **Terrain:** a modest curriculum using the existing sub-terrain machinery — flat, gentle slopes, small height-field roughness, low steps at 2b2-comparable difficulty. Enough to force real foot clearance and adaptive stepping; parkour-grade difficulty (0–1, Stage 4 geometry) is out of scope.

Retrain on the randomized environment from the Task 3 base and verify two things: robustness (gait quality and command tracking hold across the payload and terrain distributions) and no in-distribution regression (nominal-mass flat-ground metrics stay within the Task 3 ballpark).

Then produce the milestone deliverable: a before/after package — side-by-side video of the current student `9800` and the final M18 student in sim under identical command scripts (forward, backward, lateral, yaw, mixed, loaded, and light-terrain segments), plus the full metric report from the instrumented eval comparing baseline to final across every Task 1 metric. The final teacher and student weights are committed to `runs/` with READMEs per repo convention and tagged as the training baseline the sim-to-real milestone starts from.

If terrain or payload randomization destabilizes training, reduce the ranges and document the stable envelope rather than burning the milestone on curriculum research — the deliverable is a good, robust flat-and-easy-terrain gait, and the documented envelope tells the next milestone exactly what the policy can absorb.

#### Acceptance Criteria
- Payload randomization (mass range and CoM-shift range) implemented in the event config and documented, with the ranges justified against the real robot's mass uncertainty.
- Terrain curriculum (flat, slope, roughness, low-step levels with parameters) implemented and documented in the training config; Stage 4 geometry explicitly not used.
- Retrained policy holds Task 2 gait metrics and Task 3 tracking tolerance across the payload distribution; metrics vs. payload committed.
- Retrained policy traverses each terrain level with maintained gait quality; per-level metrics committed.
- No in-distribution regression: nominal-mass flat-ground metrics within the Task 3 ballpark; comparison committed.
- Teacher and student retrained; final weights committed to `runs/` with READMEs and tagged as the sim-to-real milestone's starting baseline.
- **In-sim gait demo (milestone deliverable):** side-by-side video of student `9800` vs. the final M18 student under identical command scripts spanning forward, backward, lateral, yaw, mixed, loaded, and light-terrain segments, plus the full baseline-vs-final metric report from the instrumented eval.

## FAQ
- **Why fix the gait in sim before the sim-to-real milestone rather than fine-tuning on hardware?**
  Because sim-to-real transfers whatever gait exists in sim, and iterating on reward terms takes dozens of retrains — minutes each in sim, sessions each on hardware. Hardware time in the sim-to-real milestone should be spent on the transfer gap (dynamics, sensors, latency), not on discovering that the reward function was wrong all along.
- **Why is diagnosis a whole task instead of just fixing the known suspects?**
  The two flagged suspects (the 50 ms `threshold: 0.05` air-time setting, the forward-only command model) are config observations, not measured causes, and reward shaping without measurement is how reward functions accumulate cargo-cult terms. The instrumented eval built in Task 1 is also a durable asset: every subsequent retrain in this milestone and the sim-to-real milestone gets scored on the same metrics, so regressions are caught mechanically rather than by eyeball — an upgrade over the current README-appendix, watch-the-play-run baseline process.
- **Why new reward stage classes instead of editing the 2b2 config in place?**
  The curriculum's value is that every bundled checkpoint is reproducible from its committed config (README Appendices C–G). Editing `CrabHexStage2BPhase2RewardsCfg` in place breaks reproducibility of `6300` and every stage that resumes from it. New subclasses and a new mode string extend the pattern the repo already uses for every stage transition.
- **Why a tripod-gait term — shouldn't the policy discover the gait itself?**
  It should, and if the Task 2 iteration shows tripod structure emerging from the air-time/stride/energy terms alone, the explicit term gets dropped per the remove-what-doesn't-earn-its-place rule. The term exists because the reward set is derived from a quadruped (go2) stack; a hexapod has stable-but-degenerate gait modes a quadruped reward set was never tuned to rule out, and the existing hexapod-specific terms (`penalty_excess_feet_contact_forward`, the zeroed `reward_stance_support_feet_when_forward`) constrain contact counts, not phasing.
- **Does this milestone touch the action space (prismatic vs. rotational)?**
  No. Training stays on the current rotational action space (`CrabHexDelayedJointPositionActionCfg`, scale 0.24, ±1 clip); the prismatic conversion and retrain is the sim-to-real milestone's Task 2. Keeping the action space fixed here isolates the gait changes to reward and command config, so the sim-to-real milestone's prismatic retrain starts from a known-good reward set rather than changing both at once.
- **How does this relate to the sim-to-real milestone's domain randomization, and to Stage 4?**
  This milestone randomizes only payload mass/CoM and light terrain — the parameters that shape *gait strategy* — building on the moderate push/mass/CoM DR the 2b2 stage already runs. The sim-to-real milestone's randomization pass (motor strength, PD gains, latency, sensor noise, camera pose, pushes, per the Extreme Parkour Unitree config) targets the *transfer gap* and is layered on top of this milestone's configs. Stage 4 full parkour (0.25/±4.8 actions, difficulty 0–1, full DR) remains TODO and untouched; the README's warning against jumping to `full` stands.
- **What if the fixed gait is slower than the current baseline?**
  Acceptable within reason. Some of the current forward speed comes from a degenerate gait that real lead-screw actuators cannot sustain, and the 2b2 stack's speed-forcing terms (`penalty_low_forward_speed_when_commanded` −0.8, `reward_forward_progress_along_command`) may be part of the tippy-tap cause — that's a Task 1 question. The bar is documented: forward capability at or above the `6300`/`9800` baseline where achievable, and any speed trade-off explicitly recorded with the gait-quality metrics that justify it.
- **Is a "finished" walking gait the deliverable?**
  The deliverable is a *good sim gait*: proper swing phases, tripod regularity, omnidirectional tracking, robustness to payload and light terrain, demonstrated in the before/after package against student `9800`. On-hardware gait polish and rough-terrain capability remain later work, after the sim-to-real milestone establishes transfer.
- **Where are full task details?**
  In this folder: `Task1-Gait-Diagnosis.md`, `Task2-Reward-Shaping.md`, `Task3-Omnidirectional-Tracking.md`, `Task4-Load-Terrain-Robustness.md`.
